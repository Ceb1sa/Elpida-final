What happens when you trigger SOS

Your location is captured and a pre-written emergency message is prepared. You then choose how to send it — nothing is sent automatically in the background.

  1. Your live GPS location is captured
  2. A message is generated with your name, location link & time
  3. You tap to send it to each emergency contact via WhatsApp or SMS
  4. You can call SAPS or the GBV Command Centre in one tap
  5. If you don't check in, escalate to a shareable missing-person alert
⚠️ Honest limits: this tool cannot send messages by itself in the background, contact police systems directly, or track you without your action — no app in a browser can do that safely without official integration. Every alert is sent by you, in the moment, through your phone's own WhatsApp/SMS/call apps. Set up your contacts now, before you need this.

South African stories

Real cases remembered with care. These stories discuss gender-based violence and femicide without graphic detail. Purple marks stories about women; green marks stories about men.

Woman · Femicide2019

Remembering Uyinene Mrwetyana

Uyinene was a 19-year-old University of Cape Town student. Her murder in a Cape Town post office sparked nationwide protests against violence directed at women, and the man who admitted killing her received three life sentences.

Woman · Femicide2020

Remembering Tshegofatso Pule

Tshegofatso was 28 and eight months pregnant when she was killed in Johannesburg. Her death caused widespread outrage and renewed calls for action against gender-based violence; a man later pleaded guilty to her murder and was sentenced to 20 years.

Girl · GBV2013

Remembering Anene Booysen

Anene was a 17-year-old from Bredasdorp whose rape and murder caused national outrage. A man convicted in the case received two life sentences, while her story became a painful symbol of South Africa’s fight against sexual violence.

Read the verified BBC report ↗
Man · GBV survivor2022

Phakamani Sibiya’s story

Phakamani survived a premeditated attack by his former partner in KwaZulu-Natal in 2022 and was left with permanent injuries. The woman convicted of attempting to murder him was sentenced to ten years in prison, and his story highlights the need to recognise and support male survivors of gender-based violence.

Elpida is a way for everyone to help with the current situation. South Africa is facing a huge rise in femicide and gender-based violence, and Elpida aims to tackle this by alerting everyone nearby who has the app when someone is facing danger, feels unsafe, or goes missing — sharing their description and details so their community can respond. We hope that in doing so, South Africa can become a place where everyone feels safe once again.

Why "Elpida"?

Elpida (Ελπίδα) means hope. It's what this app is built on — that a community looking out for each other can help turn the tide.

🔒 Your data

Everything you enter stays on your device — there's no server, no account, and Elpida never sees your information.
